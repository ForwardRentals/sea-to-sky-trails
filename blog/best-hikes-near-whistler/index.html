Best Hikes Near Whistler, BC

Whistler sits beside some of the best hiking in British Columbia, from a short walk to a waterfall to 28 km days in Garibaldi Provincial Park. Here is how the main hikes compare, using BC Parks and other published figures.

Easy hikes

If you want something gentle, start with the Train Wreck (about 5 km return on Whistler's official map, minimal climbing), Brandywine Falls (10 to 20 minutes each way) or Cheakamus Lake (3.5 km each way with about 90 m of gain, after a rough gravel road). See our guide to easy hikes near Whistler.

Big alpine days

The big days are in Garibaldi Provincial Park: Black Tusk (about 14 km one way), Panorama Ridge (about 14.7 km one way via Taylor Meadows) and Wedgemount Lake (6 km and 1,200 m of climbing). See big day hikes near Whistler for pacing and logistics.

Passes, dogs and safety

Several of these hikes need a free day-use pass in summer and none of the Garibaldi trails allow dogs. Read which Whistler hikes need a day pass, our dog rules guide and the safety checklist before you go.

Questions

What is the best hike near Whistler?

It depends on the day you want. Easy options include the Train Wreck, Brandywine Falls and Cheakamus Lake. For a big day, Black Tusk, Panorama Ridge and Wedgemount Lake are the classics, but each is a long, steep day.

Do I need a pass to hike near Whistler?

BC Parks requires free vehicle day-use passes in summer at Rubble Creek, Cheakamus and Diamond Head trailheads in Garibaldi Provincial Park. Confirm dates at reserve.bcparks.ca/dayuse.

Are dogs allowed on Whistler hikes?

Not in Garibaldi Provincial Park or at Rainbow Lake. Brandywine Falls allows leashed dogs. See our dog rules guide.
