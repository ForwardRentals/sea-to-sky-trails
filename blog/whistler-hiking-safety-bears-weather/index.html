Whistler Hiking Safety Checklist

Most Whistler hikes are safe for prepared people, but conditions change quickly in the Coast Mountains. Use this checklist with the land manager's current information.

Do your own research first. This article is general information, last checked November 18, 2026. Confirm conditions, passes and rules with the land manager before you go. Terms of use & disclaimer.

Before you go

  • Tell someone your route and return time (BC AdventureSmart has a free trip plan).
  • Check weather, trail conditions, closures and the avalanche forecast in snow season.
  • Book passes and reservations early.

What to carry

Water, food, navigation (a map and offline GPS), light, first aid, extra layers, sun protection, a whistle and fire or shelter. A phone alone is not a plan.

Bears

Bears live throughout the area: BC Parks says grizzly and black bears are present at Callaghan Lake and black bears and grizzlies elsewhere. Make noise, keep food secured, carry bear spray where appropriate, and give wildlife space.

Weather and daylight

BC Parks warns that Garibaldi's weather can be severe and unforgiving. Start early and set a turnaround time.

Emergencies

Call 911. Cell coverage is unreliable or missing on many trails: consider a satellite messenger.

Questions

Are there bears near Whistler?

Yes, black bears and grizzlies live in the Coast Mountains. Carry bear spray where appropriate and follow wildlife guidance.

Is there cell service on Whistler trails?

Often not. Download offline maps and tell someone your plan.
