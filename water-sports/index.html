A Field Guide · Squamish, BC

Water sports in Squamish, sorted honestly.

One town, four kinds of water: warm lakes, a glacial river, a tidal estuary, and a fjord with its own daily windstorm. Here is every way to get on it — and which one actually fits your day.

§ I  ·  The Short Version

Pick your water first, not your sport.

Most write-ups about water sports in Squamish start with gear. Start with the water instead. The lakes are warm, sheltered, and beginner-friendly. The Squamish River is cold, tidal at the bottom, and a genuine route — not a toy. Howe Sound is ocean: salt, swell, and an afternoon inflow wind strong enough to host world-cup kiteboarding. Choose the water that matches your experience and the day's weather, and every sport on this page gets easier and safer.

“The mountains don’t care how you arrived — only that you packed properly and left the place better than you found it.” House style, vol. I
§ II  ·  Flat Water

Canoe, paddleboard, kayak — the lakes.

This is where almost everyone should start. The corridor's lakes are calm by mid-morning, warm by July, and small enough that a swim back to shore is a plan, not a crisis.

  • Alice Lake — four warm lakes in one park, a sandy beach, and the easiest family canoe day on the Sea to Sky.
  • Cat Lake — small, warm, and walk-in; the gentlest first paddleboard session anywhere near town.
  • Brohm Lake — rope swings and rock ledges right off Highway 99, with a hand-carry boat launch.
  • Levette Lake — mirror water under the Tantalus; the 4×4 road keeps the crowds off it.
  • Lucille Lake — a clear, sandy-bottomed swim lake a short walk off the highway.
  • Browning Lake — the warm little lake in Murrin Park, the easiest stop on the drive up.
  • The Squamish Estuary — Skwelwil’em, a tidal bird refuge in town and the gentlest paddle in the corridor. Mind the tides and the wildlife rules.
Getting a boat You don't need roof racks. Squamish Canoe Rental delivers canoes and boards to lake parking lots across the corridor — or you can pick up and drop off at their Squamish base if you'd rather load the gear yourself. Paddles and PFDs included, up to three paddlers per canoe. The same crew runs Squamish Water Taxi — jet-boat river crossings and Howe Sound tours — for the trips where the water is the road.
§ III  ·  Moving Water

Rivers, crossings, and the paddle-to-hike.

Squamish's signature trips put the paddle at the start of something bigger. The west bank of the Squamish River has no road — so the river crossing is the trailhead.

  • Echo Falls & Echo Lake — canoe across the tidal Squamish, then climb to a thundering waterfall. Half paddle, half stair-climb.
  • Lake Lovely Water — the Tantalus cirque and the ACC hut; the approach starts with a canoe and ends 1,300 m higher.
  • River of Golden Dreams — up the highway in Whistler, the slow wetland drift from Alta Lake to Green Lake. Do it in a proper boat.
Skip the paddle entirely Squamish Water Taxi runs boat shuttles across the river and down Howe Sound — the dry-feet option for west-bank hikes, Echo Falls, and group days.
§ IV  ·  Wind & Salt

The Spit, the Sound, and the wrecks.

Kiteboarding & wing foiling

The Squamish Spit is one of the most consistent kiteboarding venues in North America. A thermal inflow wind builds over Howe Sound nearly every summer afternoon and funnels straight up the fjord — 20–35 knots of it, like clockwork. Access and launch windows on the Spit are managed seasonally; check current access rules before you go, and treat the estuary side as off-limits — it's a wildlife management area.

Ocean paddling & diving

Howe Sound is the salty end of the corridor. Porteau Cove is the closest serious saltwater launch to Vancouver, with sunken-ship reefs that make it the busiest shore-diving site in the region. Sea kayakers work the fjord's east shore; the ambitious cross to Anvil Island. Respect the same afternoon wind that the kiters drive up for — it's a headwind when you're the one paddling home.

Boat tours & rentals

Not everything on the Sound has to be earned with a paddle. Guided Howe Sound boat tours run out of Squamish — wildlife-watching trips for seals, eagles, and the fjord's cliffs, from $375 for up to three guests — and there are sunrise & sunset boat tours departing straight from Porteau Cove at the calm ends of the day. Hold a PCOC and know your way around a helm? Squamish boat rentals put you at the wheel of an 18-ft RIB to explore the fjord on your own throttle.

§ V  ·  The Practical Page

Season, wind, and cold water.

  • Season. Lakes are swimmable roughly June through September; the small, shallow ones (Cat, Browning) warm up first. The river runs cold all year — it was a glacier last week.
  • The daily wind. Calm mornings, windy afternoons, almost every sunny day. Paddle early; kite late. If you're on a lake and whitecaps appear, get off the open middle.
  • Cold water. Howe Sound and the Squamish River are cold-shock cold even in August. Wear the PFD. On the river crossings, go with a proper boat and a plan, not an inflatable.
  • Leave it better. The estuary and the river flats are working habitat — herons, eagles, spawning salmon. Distance is free; disturbance isn't.

That's the honest map of water sports in Squamish: warm lakes for learning, a river that doubles as a trailhead, and a fjord that supplies its own wind. Pick the water that fits your day, then go read the full field guide for the spot you've chosen.
