Disclaimer & safety

Please read this before using any Sea to Sky Trails guide.

Outdoor recreation is risky

Hiking, paddling, climbing, camping, swimming, diving and driving forest roads involve real risk, including serious injury and death. You are responsible for your own safety, your group and your decisions, including whether a trip is within your skills, fitness, equipment and the conditions on the day.

Our guides are general information only

Guides are compiled from public sources, which are listed at the end of each guide, and from our own field notes. They were checked on the date shown, but conditions, access, closures, fees, rules, distances and times change, sources sometimes disagree, and we can make mistakes. Where figures differ we try to show a range. Always confirm current information with the land manager (for example BC Parks, a municipality or a recreation site) before you go. Nothing here is professional, medical, legal, guiding or safety advice.

Verify before you go

  • Tell someone your plan. Leave your route, group size and expected return time with someone you trust, and check in when you are back. BC AdventureSmart (adventuresmart.ca) has a free trip-plan form.
  • Check conditions the same day. Look at the weather, road and trail conditions, closures and (in snow season) the avalanche forecast on the day you go, not last week. Use the land manager's page for this guide.
  • Set a turnaround time. Pick a time to turn back whatever happens, start early, and carry a headlamp. Most trips go wrong in the last hours of the day.
  • Carry the essentials. Water, food, navigation (map and offline GPS), light, first aid, extra layers, sun protection, a whistle and fire or shelter. A phone alone is not a plan.
  • Emergencies. Call 911. Cell coverage is unreliable or absent in much of the Sea to Sky backcountry, so consider a satellite messenger and know your location.
  • Water and wildlife. Wear a life jacket on the water: cold water can disable you in minutes. Carry bear spray where bears are present, keep food secured and give wildlife space.
Emergency: call 911. Cell coverage is unreliable or absent in much of the Sea to Sky backcountry.
