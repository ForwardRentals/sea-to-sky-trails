Shannon Falls

The easiest big waterfall in the corridor: a short walk off the highway to the third-highest waterfall in British Columbia.

Overview

BC Parks says Shannon Falls rises 335 m above Highway 99, making it the third-highest waterfall in British Columbia. A short trail along Shannon Creek leads to the viewing platforms.

The park is a popular day-use area next to the highway, with picnic areas, viewing platforms, a flush toilet building and a small concession and information centre.

Getting there

Highway 99

The park sits right on Highway 99 south of downtown Squamish. Follow posted signs for current parking arrangements.

The Chief link

BC Parks lists the distance from the Stawamus parking lot to Shannon Falls as 1.5 km, so you can combine the falls with the Stawamus Chief.

Winter

Open in winter with reduced services. Snowfall may limit access, and trail and stair treads may be slippery.

Stay safe

Keep out of the creek

BC Parks: rocks and river edges are extremely slippery, wading in the creek is extremely dangerous, and heavy rain can suddenly increase flow.

Stay behind the railings

Stay on the trail and within the viewing area.

Slippery treads

Trail and stair treads may be slippery in winter.

Dogs

Pets must be leashed. They are not allowed in beach areas or park buildings, and owners must dispose of waste.

Questions

How tall is Shannon Falls?

BC Parks says the falls rise 335 m above Highway 99, making them the third highest in British Columbia.

How long is the walk?

The easy trail along Shannon Creek is about 350 m. BC Parks gives no time, but most visitors spend well under an hour including the viewing platforms.

Can you swim at Shannon Falls?

BC Parks warns that wading in the creek is extremely dangerous and the rocks are very slippery. Stay on the trail and in the viewing area.

Is it open in winter?

Yes, with reduced services. Snowfall may limit access.
