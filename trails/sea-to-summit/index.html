Sea to Summit Trail

Overview

The Sea to Summit Trail climbs from near sea level to the summit of the Sea to Sky Gondola. The gondola's own FAQ gives the trail as 6.5 km one way, starting at 35 m and reaching 885 m, for about 918 m of gain including a few short descents.

Most people hike up and ride the gondola down to save their knees. Other sources quote different distances and times, so treat the 6.5 km gondola figure as the reference.

Getting there

Start

The trail begins at the gondola base parking lot. A source reports the first section shares the trail with the Stawamus Chief before the Sea to Summit route turns off.

Parking

Explore Squamish reports a time limit in the main lot and a free shuttle from an overflow lot at Darrell Bay. Check posted signs and the gondola website.

Tickets

We couldn't find a current official price. Check the Sea to Sky Gondola website for ticket prices and operating hours.

Know before you go

Dogs

10Adventures reports that dogs can't ride the gondola down. Confirm the current policy with the gondola before bringing a dog.

Allow the time

Estimates range from 3 to 5 hours up. Plan around daylight and gondola closing time.

Steady climb

918 m of gain over 6.5 km is a sustained climb. Bring water and layers.

When to go

Explore Squamish gives the main season for this trail as April to October. Check conditions and the gondola's operating schedule before you go.

Questions

How long is the Sea to Summit Trail?

The Sea to Sky Gondola's FAQ gives 6.5 km one way, from 35 m to 885 m elevation, about 918 m of total gain. Other sources quote 5.5 to 8 km, so use the gondola's figure as the reference.

How long does it take?

Estimates range from about 3 to 5.5 hours up, depending on fitness and stops.

Can I take the gondola down?

Yes, most hikers ride the gondola down. Check the gondola site for current tickets and hours.

Can I bring my dog?

A source reports dogs can't ride the gondola down. Confirm with the gondola before you go.

Safety checklist

Tell someone your plan

Leave your route, group size and expected return time with someone you trust, and check in when you are back. BC AdventureSmart (adventuresmart.ca) has a free trip-plan form.

Check conditions the same day

Look at the weather, road and trail conditions, closures and (in snow season) the avalanche forecast on the day you go, not last week. Use the land manager's page for this guide.

Set a turnaround time

Pick a time to turn back whatever happens, start early, and carry a headlamp. Most trips go wrong in the last hours of the day.

Carry the essentials

Water, food, navigation (map and offline GPS), light, first aid, extra layers, sun protection, a whistle and fire or shelter. A phone alone is not a plan.

Emergencies

Call 911. Cell coverage is unreliable or absent in much of the Sea to Sky backcountry, so consider a satellite messenger and know your location.

Water and wildlife

Wear a life jacket on the water: cold water can disable you in minutes. Carry bear spray where bears are present, keep food secured and give wildlife space.
