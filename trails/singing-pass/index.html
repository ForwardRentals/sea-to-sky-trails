Singing Pass

The valley trail into Garibaldi's Spearhead country: 11.5 km and about 1,200 m of climbing to Singing Pass, with Russet Lake 3 km further.

Overview

The Singing Pass Trail climbs from Whistler Village to the Singing Pass and Musical Bumps junction in Garibaldi Provincial Park. BC Parks rates it strenuous and gives it as 11.5 km one way with about 1,200 m of elevation gain.

From the junction, the trail to Russet Lake adds 3 km and 250 m. Our Russet Lake guide covers the lake and its campground in detail.

Getting there

Trailhead

BC Parks lists the Singing Pass Trail as starting from Parking Lot 5 in Whistler.

Lift option

The Musical Bumps route from the Whistler Village Gondola needs a valid lift ticket to reach the park boundary. Access to the boundary without a lift ticket is permitted only on designated routes.

Check first

Check with Whistler Blackcomb for lift openings, and check BC Parks for current notices.

Rules & safety

Harmony Creek

BC Parks: use extreme caution crossing Harmony Creek, which has no bridge.

Prohibited

Motorized vehicles, domestic animals, fires and drones are prohibited in the park year-round.

Camping

Camping needs a permit and reservation. Camping on the Spearhead Traverse requires a wilderness permit.

Weather and avalanche

BC Parks warns of severe weather and says avalanche and glacier hazards exist year-round. Travelling beyond the maintained trail needs special skills and equipment.

Pack it out

The park is user-maintained and has no garbage removal.

Questions

How long is the Singing Pass trail?

BC Parks lists it at 11.5 km one way from Parking Lot 5 to the Singing Pass and Musical Bumps junction, with about 1,200 m of elevation gain, and rates it strenuous.

Is there a bridge over Harmony Creek?

No. BC Parks says to use extreme caution when crossing Harmony Creek because there is no bridge.

Can I camp at Russet Lake?

Yes, with a reservation. BC Parks requires reservations for Russet Lake Campground year-round.

Can I bring my dog?

No. Domestic animals are prohibited in Garibaldi Provincial Park.
